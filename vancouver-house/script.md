# Vancouver House — narration script

Target: 90s at ~2.8 words/sec (~250 words). Captions are burned in one word at a time,
matching the narration.

Project: **Vancouver House**, BIG / Bjarke Ingels Group, Vancouver, Canada. 59 storeys, 149m.

---

## Beat 1 — the hook (0:00-0:07)

> This is the worst-shaped piece of land in Vancouver.
> It's a triangle. And it sits directly underneath a bridge.

## Beat 2 — the site (0:07-0:14)

> Where the Granville Bridge hits downtown, it splits into three ramps.
> The slivers of land left between them were so awkward that for decades, nobody built on them.

## Beat 3 — the rule (0:14-0:22)

> Then the city set a condition. You cannot build within thirty metres of the bridge.
> On a plot this narrow, that erases almost all of it.

## Beat 4 — the naive answer (0:22-0:29)

> So any normal tower here is a thin triangular splinter, pressed against a highway.
> Small floors. Bad apartments. Nobody wants them.

## Beat 5 — the discovery (0:29-0:38)

> But read the condition again.
> The thirty-metre setback only applies up to thirty metres of height.
> Above that line, the air is free.

## Beat 6 — the move (0:38-0:46)

> So the tower stays pinched at the bottom, obeying the rule —
> and the moment it clears the bridge, it starts leaning back out over it.

## Beat 7 — the morph (0:46-0:55)

> Floor by floor, the triangle opens. The sharp corner fattens.
> By the top, the plan is a full rectangle.

## Beat 8 — the payoff in numbers (0:55-1:02)

> The floorplate roughly doubles on the way up.
> The building takes back every square metre the bridge took from it.

## Beat 9 — what the base gives away (1:02-1:09)

> And because the base stayed thin, the ground it never used
> becomes a public plaza, right underneath the bridge.

## Beat 10 — the shadow rule (1:09-1:16)

> The curve on this side isn't sculpture. It's the sun —
> pulled back so the park next door never falls into shadow.

## Beat 11 — the read (1:16-1:23)

> Walk around it and it changes. A slab. A blade. A twist.
> It's none of those. It's a triangle becoming a rectangle.

## Beat 12 — the ancestry (1:23-1:28)

> Same trick as the Flatiron.
> Not a shape someone wanted. A shape the site allowed.

## Beat 13/14 — the reveal (1:28-1:31)

> Congratulations. You now know Vancouver House.

## Fact check

Every claim above is sourced:

- Triangular sliver site at the Granville Bridge trifurcation, previously undeveloped —
  [Metalocus](https://www.metalocus.es/en/news/vancouverism-action-vancouver-house-big),
  [Archello](https://archello.com/project/vancouver-house)
- 30m buffer from the bridge, street setbacks, and a shadow constraint protecting the
  neighbouring park — [Architectural Record](https://www.architecturalrecord.com/articles/15095-vancouver-house-by-bjarke-ingels-group)
- Setback applies only up to 30m height; above it the building expands outward,
  effectively doubling the floorplate — Architectural Record (as above)
- Triangle-to-rectangle floorplate transformation, cantilever over the site, public space
  freed at the base — [ArchEyes](https://archeyes.com/vancouver-house-by-big-architects-sustainable-urban-design-architectural-innovation/)
- Flatiron Building cited by BIG as the explicit precedent — Architizer / BIG project text
